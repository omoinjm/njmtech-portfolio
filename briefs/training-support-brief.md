## Meta Description
A look at why small business websites quietly fail after launch — and why ongoing training and support, not a single handover session, is what actually keeps a website working for the business.

## TL;DR Draft
> **TL;DR:** South African small business owners already lose an estimated 202 working days a year to admin tasks — expecting them to also self-manage their website after a single launch-day handover rarely works. The forgetting curve shows people forget up to 90% of new information within a month without reinforcement, which is exactly why one-off training fails and ongoing support succeeds.

### H2: Why Small Business Websites Quietly Fail After Launch
- Answer-first: Websites don't usually fail dramatically — they degrade slowly through stale content, missed updates, and slipped basic upkeep, because nobody was set up to catch it.
- Cover: common DIY failure modes (broken forms, outdated info, ignored security warnings); why this happens even to well-built sites

### H2: Business Owners Don't Have the Spare Time This Assumes
- Answer-first: Asking an already-stretched business owner to also learn website upkeep competes directly with time they don't have.
- Cover: admin burden already carried by SA business owners; why "just learn to do it yourself" isn't a realistic ask on top of that
- **Chart**: Bar/icon chart — 202 working days/year lost to admin, broken into task categories (accounting, invoicing, invoice processing) (Sage South Africa, citing Plum Consulting — flag as a 2018 baseline study, directional not current-state)
- **Key stat**: South African business owners lose an estimated 202 working days a year to admin tasks

### H2: Why a One-Time Handover Session Doesn't Stick
- Answer-first: A single training session at launch is set up to be forgotten — this isn't a failure of the business owner, it's how memory works without reinforcement.
- Cover: the forgetting curve; why reinforcement-based support beats a one-off walkthrough
- **Chart**: Line chart — the forgetting curve: retention over time without reinforcement, ~50% forgotten within 24 hours, ~90% within a month (Ebbinghaus, classic reference; Broad & Newstrom, 1992 training-transfer research)
- **Key stat**: People forget roughly 90% of new information within a month without reinforcement

### H2: This Isn't Unique to Websites
- Answer-first: The same pattern shows up across business software broadly — implementations fail from lack of ongoing support and training, not from bad technology.
- Cover: parallel evidence from software/ERP adoption; why this validates an ongoing-support model over a "set it and forget it" launch
- **Key stat**: More than 70% of newly implemented ERP/software initiatives are projected to fail to fully meet business-case goals by 2027, with root causes cited as behavioral, not technical (Gartner)

### H2: What Proper Ongoing Support Actually Looks Like
- Answer-first: Good support pairs a small number of technical safeguards (backups, updates, monitoring) with periodic, practical check-ins that reinforce how to use the site — not just a help-desk inbox.
- Cover: what a training-and-support retainer should include; how it differs from a pure "we fix it when it breaks" maintenance plan
- **Image**: business owner in a short training/check-in call with a support provider

### H2: South Africa's Digital-Skills Gap Makes This More Relevant, Not Less
- Answer-first: The skills gap behind this problem is structural, not a personal shortcoming of any one business owner.
- Cover: SA's digital-skills mismatch context; why ongoing support is a practical response to a documented structural gap, not overkill
- **Key stat**: South Africa faces a structural digital-skills mismatch — expanding access to education alone is not enough (World Bank, 2026)
