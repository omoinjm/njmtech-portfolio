### Introduction
- Hook: Over 75% of South Africans access the internet via mobile data — a website that isn't built mobile-first is losing visitors before they see the offer.
- Problem: Small business owners get quotes ranging from R2,000 to R80,000+ with no clear sense of what separates them.
- Promise: A transparent cost breakdown by business type, plus how to tell a good web design partner from a risky one.
- TL;DR box placement: after hook, before first H2

### H2: How South Africans Actually Browse the Web (Why This Affects Cost)
- Answer-first: Mobile-first design isn't a nice-to-have in South Africa — it's how the overwhelming majority of visitors will experience the site.
- Cover: mobile vs. fixed internet access; what "mobile-first" actually changes in a build (not just responsive CSS)
- **Chart**: Donut chart — SA mobile (75%+) vs. fixed (17.4%) internet access (ICASA, State of the ICT Sector Report 2026)
- **Key stat**: Over 75% of South Africans accessed the internet via mobile data in 2024 (ICASA, 2026)

### H2: What a Website Actually Costs in South Africa (By Business Type)
- Answer-first: Pricing splits into clear tiers by scope — landing page, small-business site, e-commerce, custom/corporate — and knowing which tier a business needs avoids over- or under-paying.
- Cover: entry landing pages (~R1,990-R2,950); typical small-business site (~R5,590-R16,900); e-commerce (from ~R7,580); hourly freelancer vs. agency rates
- **Chart**: Horizontal bar chart — SA website pricing tiers by business type (aggregated 2026 market data)
- **Key stat**: Small-business sites typically run R5,590-R16,900 once-off across current SA agency pricing (2026 market scan)

### H2: What Should Be Included at Each Price Point
- Answer-first: The price alone doesn't tell you what you're getting — scope (pages, mobile optimization, basic SEO setup, hosting handoff) is what actually matters.
- Cover: what a fair entry-tier quote includes vs. excludes; hidden costs to ask about upfront (domain, hosting, stock imagery, revisions)
- **Image**: illustration of a website build/template layout

### H2: Why Page Speed Is Part of the Cost Conversation
- Answer-first: A slow website erases the value of any design spend — speed is a conversion factor, not a technical afterthought.
- Cover: load-time impact on abandonment and conversion; why this should be part of any web design quote discussion
- **Chart**: Line chart — conversion/abandonment vs. load time at 1s/2s/3s/4s/5s (Google web.dev + Portent research)
- **Key stat**: 53% of mobile visitors abandon a page that takes longer than 3 seconds to load (Google, thinkwithgoogle.com)
- **Key stat**: A 0.1-second mobile speed improvement lifted retail conversions 8.4% (Google/Deloitte, "Milliseconds Make Millions")

### H2: How to Choose a Web Design Partner (Red Flags and Green Flags)
- Answer-first: The cheapest quote and the most expensive quote can both be wrong choices — what matters is transparency about scope, timeline, and what happens after launch.
- Cover: questions to ask before signing (who owns the code/domain, what's the maintenance plan, what's the timeline); red flags (no contract, no post-launch support, unrealistically low all-inclusive pricing with no scope detail)
- **Image**: checklist-style graphic contrasting red-flag vs. trustworthy agency signals

### H2: How Long Should a Website Take to Build?
- Answer-first: Timeline should scale with scope — a landing page and a custom e-commerce build are not the same project, and a partner who quotes both at "1 week" is a warning sign.
- Cover: realistic timelines by tier; what causes delays (content readiness, revision rounds)
